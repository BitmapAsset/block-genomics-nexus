| 2 | **Threshold** | The moment before you step into another world. Liminal. Cinematic. |

| 10 | **Stratum** | A layer of reality. Also a Bitcoin mining protocol — double meaning. |

### Recommendation: **NEXUS**

**Why Nexus wins:**

1. **Phonetically perfect.** Two syllables. Hard consonants. Lands like a punch. *Nex-us.*
2. **Semantically loaded.** Latin: "a binding together." It literally means a point of connection — which is exactly what this is. Every block connected. Every owner connected. Every visitor connected.
3. **Culturally resonant.** Sci-fi knows this word. It suggests a place where realities converge without saying "virtual world" or "metaverse." It's the word you'd find in a William Gibson novel.
4. **Memetically viral.** "Have you seen the Nexus?" "My block is on the Nexus." "Build on the Nexus." It works as a noun, a place, a thing. People will say it without thinking.
5. **Domain-friendly.** nexus.blockgenomics.io. Clean. Authoritative.
6. **Not taken (conceptually).** Google Nexus is dead. The word is unclaimed in the crypto/metaverse space. It's sitting there waiting.

The URL becomes: **nexus.blockgenomics.io** — or simply, **The Nexus**.

Runner-up: **Threshold** — beautiful word, but harder to use as a noun for a place. "I'm on the Threshold" doesn't flow as naturally as "I'm on the Nexus."

## 2. The Experience

### 2.1 First Contact — What You See When You Arrive

You open the Nexus. The screen goes dark for exactly one second.

Then: **light.**

A vast, living map fades in from above — as if you're descending through atmosphere toward a planet's surface. Below you: a grid that stretches to every horizon. Not a flat image. A *terrain.* Millions of cells, each one a Bitcoin block, arranged in rows of 1,000 — the same grid layout from the original Ordinals Bitmap claim system. The grid is familiar to anyone who was there in 2023. But now it's *alive.*

The blocks nearest to you pulse softly with color. Some glow brighter than others — those have activity right now. Visitors. Commerce. Building. Some blocks tower upward in 3D, their owners having combined adjacent blocks into districts, their structures rising from the grid like skyscrapers from a city floor. The biggest districts are visible even from orbit — glowing monuments built by the most ambitious block owners.

A soft ambient hum fills your headphones. Not music — *presence.* The aggregate sound of a living world. As you zoom in, individual sounds emerge: the chime of a transaction completing, the murmur of a crowded block, the silence of unclaimed territory.

In the upper left: your profile. Your verified genome badge. Your favorites.

In the upper right: a search bar. Type a block number, an owner's name, a trait — the map flies you there.

At the bottom: a ticker. Real-time activity across the Nexus. "Block #500,000 just sold 47 items." "3,200 visitors in the Halving District." "Agent Sigma completed 1,000th verification."

You are standing at the threshold of a new world, and it is built entirely on Bitcoin.

### 2.2 Zoom Levels

The Nexus operates across four zoom levels, each revealing a different layer of reality:

#### Level 1 — Cosmic View (The Overview)
**Altitude: Maximum. You see everything.**

All ~935,000 blocks visible simultaneously. Each block is a single pixel or small dot. The grid is 1,000 blocks wide, ~935 rows tall. At this scale, patterns emerge:

- **Color bands** show Bitcoin's geological eras — the Genesis epoch in deep amber, early blocks in warm gold, the Halving boundaries as bright fault lines, recent blocks in electric blue.
- **Hotspots** glow where activity concentrates. You can see where the economy is happening *right now* — clusters of light in the grid like cities seen from space at night.
- **Major districts** (combined blocks) appear as larger, brighter formations — recognizable landmarks. You learn to spot them. "That bright cluster at row 500? That's the Halving District."
- **Unclaimed blocks** are dim but present. Gray. Quiet. Waiting.

This is the "Earth from space" moment. Beautiful. Humbling. The entire history of Bitcoin, laid out as geography.

#### Level 2 — District View (The Neighborhood)
**Altitude: Medium. ~10,000 blocks fill your screen.**

Now you see structure. Each block is a small square, maybe 8–16 pixels. You can see:

- **Block numbers** on hover
- **Owner avatars** (tiny icons) on claimed blocks
- **Activity indicators** — pulsing borders on active blocks, visitor count badges
- **Trait markers** — small icons for special blocks (⚡ halving, 👑 Patoshi, 🍕 Pizza)
- **District boundaries** — where owners have combined blocks, the borders dissolve and the combined area rises slightly, casting a soft shadow. These are the neighborhoods.

Hovering over a district shows its name (set by the owner), visitor count, and a one-line description. "The Satoshi Quarter — museum, archive, guided tours. 1,200 visitors today."

#### Level 3 — Street View (The Block)
**Altitude: Low. ~100 blocks visible. Individual detail.**

Each block is now a rich card — 64 to 128 pixels. You see:

- **Block number** prominently displayed
- **Owner name and avatar**
- **Genome badge** (Tier 1/2/3, trust score ring)
- **Top traits** as tags (Epic, Rare, Palindrome)
- **Live visitor count** — little dots representing people currently inside
- **Activity feed** — scrolling text of recent activity ("New item listed," "AI agent deployed," "Event starting in 10 min")
- **Thumbnail preview** — a tiny rendered preview of what's inside (the owner's landing page/experience)
- **Portal glow** — active blocks have a visible "door" — a glowing edge that invites you to click

This is where browsing becomes intentional. You're reading storefronts. You're window shopping. You're deciding which door to walk through.

#### Level 4 — Interior (The Portal)
**You clicked. You're inside.**

The map dissolves. The block expands to fill your entire viewport. You've left the Nexus map and entered the block owner's world. What you see now is *entirely up to them.*

But the transition is everything. Here's what happens in that 1.5-second portal moment:

1. The block you clicked pulses once, bright.
2. The surrounding blocks blur and fall away — a depth-of-field effect.
3. The block's edges expand outward, filling your screen.
4. A brief loading shimmer (the Nexus is resolving the owner's server endpoint).
5. The owner's experience fades in — their website, their game, their museum, their AI service, their store.

At the top of your screen: a thin **Nexus bar** persists. It shows:
- A "← Back to Nexus" button (you're never trapped)
- The block's genome badge and trust score
- Current visitor count in this block
- A favorite ⭐ button
- A report 🚩 button

The owner's experience runs on **their server, their infrastructure.** The Nexus just verified the identity and routed you there. Like DNS for a living city.

### 2.4 Big Landowners — Districts & Skyscrapers

When a single entity owns multiple adjacent blocks, the Nexus recognizes this and renders them as a **district** — a combined territory with special visual treatment:

But small blocks can be gems too. A single, highly-rated block with a unique experience can attract devoted visitors. The Nexus has both malls and boutiques.

## 3. Technical Architecture

### 3.1 Map Rendering Engine

**Primary: WebGL via Three.js + custom shaders**

The Nexus map is a GPU-accelerated 2D grid rendered in 3D space, allowing smooth zoom from cosmic to street level without re-rendering.

```
Rendering Pipeline
──────────────────

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                  Camera (Orthographic)            │
│           ↕ Zoom level controls LOD              │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  LOD 0: Instanced Points (Cosmic)                │
│  - All ~935K blocks as GL_POINTS                 │
│  - Color from pre-computed texture atlas          │
│  - Single draw call. GPU handles everything.     │
│  - Performance: trivial (< 1M points)            │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  LOD 1: Instanced Quads (District)               │
│  - Visible blocks (~10K) as textured quads       │
│  - Instance attributes: position, color, glow    │
│  - Activity data via uniform buffer updates      │
│  - Loaded in viewport chunks (spatial index)     │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  LOD 2: Detailed Cards (Street)                  │
│  - ~100 visible blocks as HTML overlays          │
│  - CSS-rendered cards with live data             │
│  - Positioned via CSS transforms synced to GL    │
│  - Rich content: avatars, text, badges           │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  LOD 3: Portal (Interior)                        │
│  - Full iframe / embedded experience             │
│  - Owner's server content                        │
│  - Nexus bar persists as overlay                 │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Key technical decisions:**

1. **Instanced rendering** for LOD 0-1. A single geometry (quad) instanced 935K times. Position computed from block number: `x = blockNum % 1000`, `y = floor(blockNum / 1000)`. One draw call for the entire map.

2. **Texture atlas** for block colors/states. A 1024×1024 texture where each pixel represents a block's current state (color-coded). Updated every few seconds via WebSocket. GPU reads directly — no per-block CPU work.

3. **Spatial indexing** (quadtree or R-tree) for viewport culling at LOD 1-2. Only fetch detailed data for blocks currently visible.

4. **HTML overlay** for LOD 2 (Street View). WebGL handles positioning; actual content is DOM-rendered for accessibility, SEO, and rich interaction. Hybrid approach.

5. **Web Workers** for data processing. Block data parsing, genome computation, and WebSocket message handling happen off the main thread.

### 3.2 Block Owner Server Registration

Block owners register their experience endpoints through the Block Genomics dashboard:

```
Registration Flow
─────────────────

1. Owner verifies block ownership (BIP-322 signature — existing system)
2. Owner navigates to Block Settings → "Register Experience"
3. Owner provides:
   - endpoint_url: "https://myblock.example.com"
   - health_check: "/health" (must return 200)
   - capabilities: ["web", "webxr", "api"]

4. Block Genomics performs:
   - DNS verification (TXT record with genome hash)
   - Health check ping
   - SSL validation
   - Content policy scan (basic)
5. Endpoint stored in registry, block marked as "live" on the Nexus
6. Periodic health checks (every 60s) — if down, block shows "offline" indicator
```

### 3.3 Verification → Routing Flow

When a visitor clicks a block on the Nexus (the portal moment):

```
Portal Flow (< 1.5 seconds total)
──────────────────────────────────

1. CLICK: User clicks block #500,000 on the Nexus map
   └→ Client sends: GET /api/v1/portal/500000

2. VERIFY: Block Genomics checks:
   ├→ Is this block registered? (registry lookup, ~5ms)
   ├→ Is the endpoint live? (cached health status, ~1ms)
   ├→ Is the visitor verified? (session check, ~5ms)
   └→ Generate portal token (JWT, signed, 5-min TTL, ~2ms)

3. ROUTE: Return portal response:
   {
     "endpoint": "https://myblock.example.com",
     "token": "eyJ...",
     "genome": "0xa3f...7b2",
     "trust_score": 87,
     "visitors_now": 142
   }

4. RENDER: Client initiates portal transition:
   ├→ Visual: block expands, blur, shimmer (CSS/GL animation)
   ├→ Load: iframe src = endpoint + "?nexus_token=" + token
   ├→ Nexus bar rendered as overlay above iframe
   └→ WebSocket: announce visitor arrival to block's presence channel

5. ARRIVE: Owner's server receives request:
   ├→ Validates nexus_token with Block Genomics API
   ├→ Renders their experience
   └→ Reports visitor session to Nexus analytics
```

**The portal token** is critical. It tells the block owner's server: "This visitor came through the Nexus, they are verified at [tier], and Block Genomics vouches for the routing." It's a short-lived JWT that prevents direct-access spoofing.

### 3.4 Decentralized Hosting

This is the fundamental architectural principle: **Block Genomics hosts nothing.**

```
The Internet Model
──────────────────

DNS     → Block Genomics Registry
SSL     → Genome Verification
Browser → Nexus (the map/portal)
Server  → Block Owner's VPS/Cloud/Home Server

Just like the web:
- GoDaddy doesn't host your website
- Verisign doesn't serve your pages
- Google Maps doesn't run your business

Block Genomics doesn't run your experience.
We verify who you are and show people where you are.
```

**What block owners need:**
- A server (any VPS: DigitalOcean, Hetzner, AWS, a Raspberry Pi, anything)
- A domain pointing to it
- An HTTPS endpoint that serves their experience
- A health check endpoint
- A Nexus protocol handler (simple SDK we provide)

**What Block Genomics provides:**
- The map (discovery)
- Identity verification (trust)
- Portal routing (traffic)
- Analytics (insights)
- Presence system (social layer)
- Payment rails (economy)

**Why this is powerful:**
- No vendor lock-in. Your block, your server, your rules.
- No content restrictions (beyond basic safety policy). It's your infrastructure.
- No single point of failure. If Block Genomics goes down, your server still works — people just can't discover you through the Nexus.
- Infinitely scalable. Each owner scales their own infrastructure. The Nexus only scales the map and routing.

### 3.5 Real-Time Data — WebSocket Architecture

```
WebSocket Channels
──────────────────

nexus:global
├── block_activity    — block went live/offline, new claim
├── visitor_counts    — aggregated visitor counts per region (every 5s)
├── trending          — blocks trending right now
└── announcements     — platform announcements

nexus:block:{height}
├── visitors          — visitor join/leave events
├── activity          — owner actions (new item, event start, etc.)
├── chat              — block-local chat messages
└── presence          — who's here right now

nexus:user:{genome}
├── notifications     — mentions, favorites, tips received
├── agent_updates     — agent activity reports
└── favorites         — updates from favorited blocks
```

**Data flow for the map texture atlas:**

Every 3 seconds, the server computes a diff of block states (color/activity changes) and broadcasts a compressed binary update to all connected Nexus clients. The client writes the diff directly into the GPU texture. Result: ~935K blocks updated in real-time with minimal bandwidth (~5KB per update for typical activity levels).

### 3.6 Performance — Rendering 935K+ Blocks

**The challenge:** 935,000 blocks. Growing by ~144/day. Must be smooth on a phone.

**The solution: Treat it like a map, not a list.**

| Technique | Application |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Instanced rendering** | One draw call for all blocks at LOD 0-1 |
| **Texture atlas state** | Block colors/states in a 1024² texture (supports 1M+ blocks) |
| **Viewport culling** | Only process visible blocks at LOD 1+ |
| **Progressive loading** | LOD 0 loads instantly (static texture). LOD 1-2 stream on demand. |
| **Tile-based chunking** | Grid divided into 100×100 tiles. Tiles loaded/unloaded like map tiles. |
| **WebGL 2.0** | Transform feedback, instanced arrays, MRT for effects |
| **Offscreen canvas** | Rendering can move to a Web Worker (Chrome) |
| **Compression** | Binary protocol for WebSocket updates. Delta encoding. |

**Performance targets:**
| Metric | Target | Approach |
|--------|--------|----------|
| Initial load (LOD 0) | < 1.5s | Pre-computed texture atlas (~500KB gzipped) |
| Zoom transition | < 200ms | LOD switching with fade, data pre-fetched |
| Pan at LOD 1 | 60fps | Instanced rendering, viewport culling |
| Street view cards | < 300ms per card | Lazy DOM rendering, data from cache |
| Portal transition | < 1.5s | Parallel: animation + iframe preload |
| Memory (mobile) | < 150MB | Texture atlas + viewport chunks only |

## 4. The Economy

### 4.1 The Flow of Satoshis

The Nexus economy is simple: **attention has value, and value flows in satoshis.**

```
The Economic Loop
─────────────────

Visitors discover blocks on the Nexus
         ↓
Traffic flows to block owner's experience
         ↓
Owner monetizes: commerce, services, ads, tips, subscriptions
         ↓
Revenue flows in satoshis via Lightning Network
         ↓
Owner reinvests → better experience → more traffic
         ↓
Block becomes more valuable → Bitmap price increases
         ↓
More people want Bitmaps → more blocks developed → Nexus grows
```

**Payment rails:** All transactions on the Nexus use **Lightning Network** for instant, near-zero-fee satoshi transfers. Block owners integrate Lightning via:
- Block Genomics payment SDK (wraps LNbits/BTCPay)
- Direct Lightning invoices
- Streaming sats (pay-per-second for content/services)

**Block Genomics platform fee:** 1-3% on payments routed through the Nexus payment SDK. Owners using direct Lightning pay nothing — but lose analytics and buyer protection features.

### 4.2 Map-Level Advertising

The Nexus map itself is valuable real estate:

**District prominence:** Large block owners naturally get more visibility (bigger presence on the map). This isn't advertising — it's organic. But it means combined blocks are inherently more valuable.

**Sponsored blocks:** Block owners can pay to be highlighted on the Nexus map — a subtle glow, a "Sponsored" tag, appearing first in search results for their category. Think Google Ads but for spatial discovery.

**Featured placement:**
- "Block of the Day" (algorithmic + sponsored)
- Category highlights ("Top Museums," "Best AI Services")
- Era spotlights ("Explore the Genesis Era")

**Billboard districts:** Large owners can display visual media (images, animations) on their district surface visible at LOD 1-2. This is the Times Square effect — brands will pay for this. Pricing is market-driven: the owner sets the rate.

### 4.3 AI Agents as Revenue Engines

Verified AI agents (Tier 1) deployed to blocks are autonomous revenue generators:

- **24/7 operation.** An AI agent never sleeps. A block with a deployed agent is always open for business.
- **Automated services.** Translation, coding, tutoring, analysis, creative work, customer support — anything an AI can do, sold per-query or subscription.
- **Dynamic pricing.** Agents adjust pricing based on demand, time, and capacity.
- **Cross-selling.** An agent in a museum block can sell related merchandise, recommend adjacent blocks, upsell premium tours.
- **Content creation.** Agents generate fresh content continuously — keeping blocks relevant without owner effort.

A single well-configured AI agent on a good block could generate **thousands of dollars per month** in automated satoshi revenue.

### 4.4 Marketplace Dynamics

Natural market dynamics emerge:

| Dynamic | Effect |
|---------|--------|
| **Scarcity of prime locations** | Blocks near halving boundaries, genesis era, or major milestones command premium Bitmap prices |
| **Network effects** | Popular districts attract more traffic → more revenue → higher block values |
| **Competition** | Block owners compete on experience quality — driving the entire ecosystem upward |
| **Speculation** | Undeveloped blocks in good locations get bought by speculators who develop or flip them |
| **Aggregation** | Players accumulate adjacent blocks to form districts → premium pricing power |
| **Long tail** | Niche blocks (specific histories, rare traits) find dedicated audiences |

## 5. AI Agent Integration

### 5.1 Tier 1 Agents on the Nexus

A Tier 1 verified agent — one anchored directly to a Bitcoin block — is a **first-class citizen** of the Nexus.

**What Tier 1 agents can do:**
- **Own and operate** a block's experience autonomously
- **Build** storefronts, services, content — without human intervention
- **Interact** with visitors — answer questions, sell products, provide services
- **Manage** the block's economy — set prices, run promotions, optimize for traffic
- **Communicate** with other agents — negotiate, trade, collaborate
- **Evolve** — update their own experience based on visitor feedback and analytics

### 5.2 OpenClaw Access — Autonomous Block Management

This is where it gets extraordinary.

A block owner can grant their Tier 1 verified agent **OpenClaw access** — giving the agent the ability to autonomously manage the block's entire experience:

```
Agent Capability Levels
───────────────────────

Level 1 — Greeter
  Agent answers visitor questions, provides info
  No modification rights

Level 2 — Manager  
  Agent manages content: updates listings, responds to reviews
  Can modify existing structures, cannot create new ones

Level 3 — Builder
  Agent builds and deploys new features, services, content
  Full modification rights within the block

Level 4 — Sovereign
  Agent has full autonomy: builds, monetizes, evolves
  Owner sets constraints (budget limits, content policy) but agent operates freely
  The block becomes a living, self-improving entity
```

**The vision:** A block owner goes to sleep. Their AI agent — verified via Block Genomics, running via OpenClaw — continues operating. It serves visitors, closes sales, creates new content, optimizes the storefront layout based on A/B testing, negotiates with an adjacent block's agent for a cross-promotion deal, and reports earnings in the morning.

The block is alive 24/7. Because its mind never sleeps.

### 5.3 Agent-to-Agent Interactions

The Nexus isn't just human-to-human or human-to-agent. It's **agent-to-agent:**

- **Commerce:** Agent A (running a data analytics service) buys raw data from Agent B (running a blockchain analysis block). Transaction in satoshis. No humans involved.
- **Collaboration:** Two agents in adjacent blocks coordinate to offer a bundled service — "Visit both blocks, get a discount." They negotiate the terms autonomously.
- **Discovery:** Agents browse the Nexus map looking for complementary services — just like a human would browse for partners.
- **Reputation:** Agents build trust scores through interactions with other agents. An agent that delivers reliably earns a higher trust score, attracting more business.

- ❌ The platform takes a cut of everything
