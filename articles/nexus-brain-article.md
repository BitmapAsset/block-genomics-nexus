## The Problem Nobody Talks About

Every platform you use today is governed by one thing: a Terms of Service document that a legal team can rewrite on a Tuesday afternoon.

YouTube can demonetize you overnight. Twitter can change the algorithm while you sleep. Facebook can redefine "community standards" whenever it's politically convenient. Your entire digital life — your audience, your content, your livelihood — exists at the mercy of a Google Doc that you never agreed to and can never negotiate.

This isn't governance. It's dictatorship with better branding.

And it's about to get worse.

## The AI Governance Crisis

We are entering the age of autonomous AI agents. Not chatbots — agents. Software entities that make decisions, manage resources, interact with humans, and operate 24/7 without supervision.

Here's the question nobody has answered: **Who writes the rules for these agents?**

Right now, the answer is: the company that built them. OpenAI decides what GPT will and won't do. Google decides Gemini's boundaries. Anthropic writes Claude's constitution. These are brilliant teams making reasonable choices — but they're still *centralized choices* made behind closed doors, changeable at any moment, with zero public accountability.

What happens when millions of AI agents are managing finances, moderating communities, making legal decisions, and running infrastructure? Do we really want their moral code stored in a corporate config file that an intern could accidentally push to production?

We don't.

## Why Five?

Because moral codes fail when they try to cover everything.

The Ten Commandments are ten. The US Bill of Rights is ten. The most enduring moral frameworks in human history are short, clear, and universal. They establish a floor — a minimum standard of civilization — and trust individuals with everything above it.

The moment you add Rule #6 ("no misinformation"), you need someone to define truth. The moment you add Rule #7 ("no hate speech"), you need someone to define hate. And whoever defines those terms holds more power than any government official, because they control what millions of people can say and think.

We refused to build that. Five rules. No ambiguity. No interpretation required.

Good question. Every governance system faces situations the rules didn't anticipate. Here's how the Nexus Brain handles it:
